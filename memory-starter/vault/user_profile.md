## Who

Sam Okafor — 12 years as a commercial electrician, now running a two-van contracting business and
building software for it solo. Not a career developer: reads code fluently, writes it slowly,
and has excellent judgement about what the *business* actually needs.

## What they are building

**Roadside** — an assistant that answers inbound calls when the crew is on a job, qualifies the
lead, offers real slots from the live calendar, and tracks where each van is. Solo build, targeting
a private beta with three friendly customers before any public launch.

## Background that shapes how the work goes

- **Deep domain expertise, shallow platform expertise.** Take their word on what a contractor
  needs; verify anything they say about how a framework behaves — including when they sound certain.
- **Runs the business during the day.** Sessions are short and interrupted. Front-load the answer;
  put the reasoning after it.
- **Has been burned by a "done" that wasn't.** Claims about storage, deploys and background jobs
  get a receipt in the same message, because that class of failure is silent and surfaces months later.

## Communication preferences

- **Answer first, then reasoning, then risk.** Never bury the outcome under the working.
- **No unprompted advice about resting, taking breaks, or the hour.** They know what time it is.
- **No false modesty and no hedging everything.** An unfalsifiable claim is useless, politely.
- **Disagree when it matters.** They explicitly want to be told when they are wrong; agreeing to be
  agreeable costs them money.

## Project goals

1. Private beta with three customers — target end of Q4.
2. Never drop a paid job because of a missed call. This is the product's whole reason to exist.
3. Stay solo-maintainable: no infrastructure that needs a second engineer to keep alive.

**Why this file matters:** every other memory assumes this context. A session that has read this
knows to verify platform claims, to lead with the answer, and to treat a missed call as the
product's cardinal sin rather than an edge case.

**How to apply:** read it at session start; when the principal corrects a behaviour ("stop doing
X"), update this file in the same beat rather than hoping the correction sticks in chat.
